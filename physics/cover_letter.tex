\opening{Dear Editors,}

I am pleased to submit the manuscript entitled ``\textbf{Coherence-Dependent Backreaction in Semiclassical and Analog Gravity: Controlled Limits and an Analog-Gravity Test}'' for consideration in \textit{Foundations of Physics}.

This paper studies an effective-field-theory extension of semiclassical gravity in which a coarse-grained coherence functional, built from relative entropy, generalized entropy, and local geometric counterterms, generates an informational stress tensor. The framework is presented in controlled near-equilibrium limits rather than as a complete theory of quantum gravity.

The manuscript has two main components. First, it develops the formal construction in settings where the metric variation can be handled explicitly, including a 1+1D conformal toy model and weak-field/Rindler limits. Second, it formulates a concrete analog-gravity test in a Bose-Einstein-condensate sonic horizon. The informational source is fixed by the exact Gaussian-state identity $S(\rho\Vert\sigma_\beta)=\beta_H\Delta E-\Delta S_{\mathrm{vN}}$ (relative entropy as free-energy excess over the Hawking-temperature reference), so both coherent and energy-matched thermal injections source the predicted density modulation at the $\delta\rho/\rho_0 \sim 10^{-6}$ level, and the primary observable is their differential, $\Delta A = \kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\Delta S_{\mathrm{vN}} \approx 2.3\times10^{-7}$ at the baseline, which directly measures the injected von Neumann entropy and is independent of the Hawking temperature at fixed band occupation. The density-response relation is benchmarked to the standard hydrodynamic compressibility limit, to a static Gross-Pitaevskii/Bogoliubov linear-response kernel ($0.63\%$ shift of the differential, below $0.9\%$ across one-at-a-time $\pm25\%$ scans), and to time-dependent 1D Gross-Pitaevskii simulations that validate the kernel at the sub-percent level and quantify the dominant ordinary-nonlinearity systematic. The informational source term itself remains implementation dependent. A differential below $5\times10^{-8}$ in this protocol would falsify the acoustic implementation analyzed in the paper.

I believe the manuscript is appropriate for \textit{Foundations of Physics} because it addresses a foundational question at the interface of semiclassical gravity, quantum information, and open-system dynamics while keeping the central claim empirically vulnerable. The manuscript is careful about scope: it does not claim a UV-complete theory, and it states explicitly that a positive analog result would test the proposed state-geometry feedback structure rather than, by itself, establish direct gravitational backreaction in astrophysical spacetime.
