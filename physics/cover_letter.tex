\opening{Dear Editors,}

I am pleased to submit the manuscript entitled ``\textbf{Coherence-Dependent Backreaction in Semiclassical and Analog Gravity: Controlled Limits and an Analog-Gravity Test}'' for consideration in \textit{Foundations of Physics}.

This paper studies an effective-field-theory extension of semiclassical gravity in which a coarse-grained coherence functional, built from relative entropy, generalized entropy, and local geometric counterterms, generates an informational stress tensor. The framework is presented in controlled near-equilibrium limits rather than as a complete theory of quantum gravity.

The manuscript has two main components. First, it develops the formal construction in settings where the metric variation can be handled explicitly, including a 1+1D conformal toy model and weak-field/Rindler limits. Second, it formulates a concrete analog-gravity test in a Bose-Einstein-condensate sonic horizon. Within the acoustic implementation studied here, coherent phonon injection produces a predicted density modulation of order $\delta\rho/\rho_0 \sim 10^{-6}$ near the horizon, whereas a matched thermal control state gives no leading-order informational contribution. The density-response relation is benchmarked to the standard hydrodynamic compressibility limit of Gross-Pitaevskii/Bogoliubov theory, while the informational source term itself remains implementation dependent. A null result below $10^{-7}$ in this protocol would falsify the acoustic implementation analyzed in the paper.

I believe the manuscript is appropriate for \textit{Foundations of Physics} because it addresses a foundational question at the interface of semiclassical gravity, quantum information, and open-system dynamics while keeping the central claim empirically vulnerable. The manuscript is careful about scope: it does not claim a UV-complete theory, and it states explicitly that a positive analog result would test the proposed state-geometry feedback structure rather than, by itself, establish direct gravitational backreaction in astrophysical spacetime.
